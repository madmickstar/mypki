Inspect a PEM

Paste a certificate or a certificate signing request and read what it actually says: subject, issuer, SANs, key and its size, validity window, serial, fingerprint and signature algorithm — plus the things that matter out loud, like expired, self-signed, not a CA or signed with SHA-1. This page never refuses anything and never changes anything: it is a reader, not a verifier.

What this page cannot tell you

  • It does not verify a chain. There is no trust store on this host and no issuer certificate to check against, so it can name an issuer but cannot say that anybody trusts it.
  • It does not know about revocation. This page reads the document you pasted and consults nothing else: not this host's CRL, not /ocsp, not any other CA's. A certificate reported as valid here may still have been revoked by whoever issued it, and a certificate from another CA is not affected by what this host published at all.
  • Its findings are not gates. A SHA-1 signature or an RSA-1024 key is written down rather than refused. The rules that do refuse live on Sign a certificate, which is the page that issues.
  • A private key is named and not opened. Paste one by accident and this page says it is a key, displays none of it, and does not attempt to decrypt it.
